False news spreads faster than corrections, and readers in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana meet it in both English and Telugu, often next to a convincing AI-generated image. Most automatic detectors handle only English text, give one label for a whole article and do not explain it. This project presents TruthLens AI, a web application that classifies English and Telugu news, detects AI-generated images, explains every verdict, and checks the individual claims in an article against evidence.

English news is classified by DeBERTa-v3-large and Telugu news by IndicBERT v2, both fine-tuned on a free T4 GPU. The English model was trained on the WELFake dataset after its reversed labels were corrected and publisher names were removed; because the public Telugu dataset could be separated at 99.8% by word counts alone, a Telugu set was built by translating WELFake with NLLB-200. Images are scored by the average of two detectors: a linear head on frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 features trained in this project, and a public Swin-V2 detector trained on recent generators. Occlusion marks the words behind each text verdict and Grad-CAM shows the image regions behind each image verdict. For claim verification, the article is split into check-worthy claims. An open-weight language model, gpt-oss-120b, writes search queries for each claim; evidence is gathered from Wikipedia, the web, Google News, published fact-checks and a local corpus; multilingual-E5 keeps the most relevant passages; and the language model labels the claim Supported, Contradicted or Unknown, gives a one-line reason and cites the passages it used. When no language model is available, a natural language inference model with a contradiction-first rule takes over.

The English classifier reached 99.7% accuracy on 12,727 test articles, against 98.8% for BERT-base, and the Telugu classifier reached 93.1%, against 87.1% for a TF-IDF baseline. The CLIP detector reached 93.7% on CIFAKE, and the two-detector average judged 50 of 61 recent and CIFAKE images correctly, against 41 for the CLIP detector alone. On 41 single-sentence claims run through the whole application, the language-model checker gave the right verdict for all 41, each with a cited source, while the NLI pipeline got 35 right and left 4 unverified. With gold evidence, the NLI rule reached 84.6% accuracy on FEVER and 50.9% on Climate-FEVER, against 40.0% and 34.0% for a similarity-only baseline that found no contradictions. Occlusion highlights were about ten times faster than LIME and as faithful to the classifier. The system is built with FastAPI and React, keeps a history of full reports, and installs and starts with one command.

**Keywords:** fake news detection, Telugu, DeBERTa, IndicBERT, CLIP, explainable AI, claim verification, large language models, retrieval-augmented fact-checking.
