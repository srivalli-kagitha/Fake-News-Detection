False news spreads faster than corrections, and readers in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana meet it in both English and Telugu, often next to a convincing AI-generated image. Most automatic detectors handle only English text, give one label for a whole article and do not explain it. This project presents TruthLens AI, a web application that classifies English and Telugu news, detects AI-generated images, explains every verdict, and checks the individual claims in an article against evidence.

English news is classified by DeBERTa-v3-large and Telugu news by IndicBERT v2, both fine-tuned on a free T4 GPU. The English model was trained on the WELFake dataset after its reversed labels were corrected and publisher names were removed; because the public Telugu dataset could be separated at 99.8% by word counts alone, a Telugu set was built by translating WELFake with NLLB-200. Images are classified by a linear head on frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 features. LIME marks the words behind each text verdict and Grad-CAM shows the image regions behind each image verdict. For claim verification, the article is split into check-worthy claims, evidence is retrieved with BGE-M3 embeddings and FAISS, and a natural language inference model labels each claim Supported, Contradicted or Unknown, with contradiction tested first.

The English classifier reached 99.7% accuracy on 12,727 test articles, against 98.8% for BERT-base, and the Telugu classifier reached 93.1%, against 87.1% for a TF-IDF baseline. The image detector reached 93.7% on CIFAKE. On FEVER, claim verification reached 84.6% accuracy and 0.847 macro-F1, while a similarity-only baseline reached 40.0% and found no contradictions; on the harder, real-world Climate-FEVER set it reached 50.9%, against 34.0% for the baseline. The system is built with FastAPI and React, supports batch checking and history, and installs and starts with one command.

**Keywords:** fake news detection, Telugu, DeBERTa, IndicBERT, CLIP, explainable AI, claim verification, natural language inference.
